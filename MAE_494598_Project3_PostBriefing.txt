
Project 3 Post Interview Briefing
MAE 598 | 10/19/2026
Dylan Brandt, Leo Dayal, Tyler Bay 
	The focus of the interview was the creation of optimal metasurfaces that work in relation to beamforming to manipulate electromagnetic waves (for example, in their direction or power) without the use of mechanical parts. This works by changing the geometry of the surface as a means to focus or disperse an already existing signal that passes into it, allowing for more dynamic and customizable systems capable of wider use cases than current technology. The applications of this technology are vast within communication sectors, as beam focusing could allow for larger bandwidth and obstacle permeation of 5G and 6G radio signals, meaning service would improve through the development of virtual line of sight technology, increasing both speed and coverage. However, Nathan is specifically working in power dispersion, which allows for signals that charge devices to be sent over the air and to a device. This would have a key role in various technological sectors, such as medical devices that require electricity and currently need to be removed to sustain operation for long periods of time, and general Internet of Things devices that would become easier to power and charge.
	This system is governed by the physics Maxwell’s equations,  a set of four differential equations that govern the principles of electromagnetism, which is used to determine the effectiveness of a given metasurface in transferring power across it in a desired configuration. These equations include Gauss’ Laws for Electricity and Magnetism, Faraday’s Law, and the Ampere-Maxwell Law. The current solver used is Ansys Electronic Desktop, in which they use the HFSS (High-Frequency Structure Simulator) and Nexxim to solve for metasurface characteristics. In addition, some optimization work is typically done on the pre-solving end to give the solver values that are believed to produce ideal results. One solve is typically completed on the order of hours, and it can take anywhere from 50-100 individual iterations to optimize the metasurface. Specific challenges include the amount of time it takes to optimize a metasurface (those 50-100 individual iterations are performed over the course of multiple months), as well as the fact that failure modes (what causes certain metasurfaces to not meet the desired performance) is not very well understood.
	While Nathan does not work with any PINN or PINO at this time, he mentioned that others in his lab have implemented it to meet performance deliverables. The application of this type of Machine Learning has been successful thus far, with it performing orders of magnitude quicker than traditional solving methods (for example, the manual solving carried out by Nathan). Primarily, this was related to decreasing the time required to optimize a surface because simulations could be carried out frequently and consistently without human intervention.
